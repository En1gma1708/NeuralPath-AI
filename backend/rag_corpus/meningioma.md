# Meningioma — Clinical Overview

## Epidemiology

Meningiomas represent approximately 37.6% of central nervous system tumors,
with an incidence of 9.73 per 100,000 people annually in the United States
(2016-2020). The mean presentation age is 66 years, with a female-to-male
ratio of 2.3:1. The majority of meningiomas are benign and classified as WHO
grade 1 tumors. Among older adults (75-84 years), incidence reaches 37.75
per 100,000, contrasting sharply with 0.14 per 100,000 in children aged
0-19 years.

## Clinical Presentation

Manifestations vary by location. Parasagittal tumors may cause contralateral
limb paresis, while sphenoid ridge lesions can produce cranial nerve
palsies. Olfactory groove meningiomas can lead to Foster-Kennedy syndrome,
which causes anosmia, contralateral papilledema, and ipsilateral optic
nerve atrophy. Spinal meningiomas typically present with pain and
radiculopathy. Many patients remain asymptomatic, with tumors discovered
incidentally.

## Imaging Characteristics

Brain MRI is the diagnostic standard. On unenhanced sequences, meningiomas
typically appear hypointense on T1-weighted and hyperintense on
T2-weighted imaging. Post-contrast imaging reveals homogeneous enhancement
with the characteristic dural tail sign, distinguishing extra-axial from
intra-axial lesions. The cerebrospinal fluid vascular cleft — CSF trapped
between tumor and cortex — further supports diagnosis. Head CT excels at
detecting calcifications and hyperostosis.

## WHO Classification

Distribution follows: 80.1% grade 1 (benign), 18.3% grade 2 (atypical), and
1.5% grade 3 (malignant). Grade 1 tumors include nine histological variants
with benign features. Grade 2 tumors demonstrate increased cellularity,
prominent nuclei, or 4-19 mitoses per 10 high-power fields. Grade 3 lesions
show anaplasia, high mitotic activity (≥20 mitoses/10 high-power fields),
and aggressive behavior.

## Management

Asymptomatic, small, and slow-growing meningiomas are usually treated with
observation and routine imaging. Symptomatic or enlarging tumors warrant
surgical resection, with the Simpson grading system predicting recurrence
based on resection extent. Gross total resection provides >90% three-year
progression-free survival for benign tumors. Adjuvant radiotherapy improves
outcomes in grade 2-3 disease, while stereotactic radiosurgery offers a
non-surgical alternative.
